# CASMI 2026 (Enveda) data release: contents and the test placeholder

> **Status:** FINDING · **Updated:** 2026-10-06
> Downloaded and extracted; nothing preprocessed. Next: decide the train filter. The visible `test.parquet` is a placeholder copied verbatim from `train.parquet`, as the
> competition intends (§3). Score only on a molecule-disjoint split of train. Training-data
> checks (§5): labels are consistent; 166k high-resolution spectra are poorly explained by
> their labelled molecule, 70% of them RIKEN rows labelled plain `QTOF`/`Orbitrap`. The
> Spectraverse dedup rule drops 17.6% of train (§5.5), and 2,022 multi-peak spectra are filed
> under two molecules. Later sections: MSnLib vs NIST20/23 overlap (§5.7), natural-product
> distribution bias of train (§5.8), and what the `riken` library is (§5.9).

## 1. Source and task

- Kaggle competition [Enveda CASMI 2026 - Molecule ID From Mass Spectra](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra),
  2026-09-14 to 2026-12-14 (entry deadline 2026-12-07).
- Local copy: `data/raw/casmi2026/enveda-CASMI26-molecule-id-mass-spectra.zip`
  (3,032,290,079 bytes, uploaded 2026-10-05, zip CRC check passes), extracted alongside it:
  `train.parquet` (3.03 GB), `test.parquet` (4.8 MB), `sample_submission.csv`.
- Task: up to 25 ranked SMILES per `molecule_id`, `;`-separated, in a `molecule_id,smiles`
  CSV. Metric: MRR@25 on the connectivity block of a tautomer-canonical InChIKey.
- Submission is a Kaggle notebook. A scored rerun has internet off and replaces the visible test
  with the hidden one: about 400 molecules and 1,500 unpublished timsTOF spectra of natural
  products (competition overview).
- pyarrow 25.0.1 was added to `FRAGNNET-GPU` on 2026-10-05 (user approved; numpy stays 1.26.4)
  so the project environment reads the parquet files.

  `[M+K]+` 2, `[M+Cl]-` 2. 97 molecules have spectra in both polarities, and 108 have more
  than one adduct.
- Peaks are unfiltered: median 230 per spectrum, smallest relative intensity 2e-7. 706 spectra
  have a peak above precursor + 0.5.
- Precursor m/z 245-460.

## 5. Training-data checks

The checks of `notebooks/data_notebooks/nist20.ipynb`, `nist23.ipynb` and
`nist23_data_quality.ipynb`, with subformula coverage in place of DAG coverage (no DAGs built).

- **Script:** [`scripts/debug/casmi26/casmi26_train_stats.py`](../../scripts/debug/casmi26/casmi26_train_stats.py),
  13 min on 6 CPU workers. It writes `output/casmi26_train_check_2026_10_05/`:
  `casmi26_train_spec_stats.parquet` (one row per train row),
  `casmi26_train_mol_stats.parquet` (one row per SMILES) and `run_info.json`. The run was at
  `6f2fdea2` with a dirty tree (this script was uncommitted).
- **Notebook:** `notebooks/data_notebooks/casmi26_train.ipynb`, executed, reads those tables.
- **Instrument classes.** The free-text instrument labels are mapped to four classes: TOF
  1,493,117 spectra, ORB (Orbitrap and other FT) 950,640, LR (ion trap, QQQ, sector) 61,110 and
  UNK 34,741.

### 5.1 Labels

- Every normalized SMILES parses in RDKit. RDKit's formula equals `molecular_formula` for all
  277,566. No SMILES carries two formulas, none has two components, and 626 are charged.
- `num_peaks` equals the length of `ms2_mzs` for every spectrum.

### 5.2 Precursor m/z

- The error is recomputed from formula and adduct for 21 adduct strings; dimers use 2M. It
  covers all but 6,840 spectra.
- pluskal_ms2, MassBank, MoNA, Spectraverse, MS-DIAL and drug_plus have median errors of
  0.06-0.12 ppm. They store the theoretical precursor m/z, so for them this check catches gross
  errors but cannot confirm an adduct.
- Share of spectra with error above 10 ppm, then above 0.5 Da: RIKEN 25.2% and 3.2%, MassBank
  9.9% and 1.4%, GNPS 7.5% and 4.7%, MoNA 6.5% and 2.2%, drug_plus 3.2% and 3.2%. Enveda,
  pluskal, Spectraverse, MS-DIAL, masaryk and np-examples are at 0.2% or less.
- The release's `precursor_error_ppm` equals ours only for `enveda-np-examples`. Its Spearman
  correlation with ours is 0.02 for Enveda, pluskal and MS-DIAL, and -0.16 to -0.38 elsewhere,
  so it is a different quantity. Do not use it as a formula check.
- **Precision.** 85% of the RIKEN rows labelled plain `QTOF` or `Orbitrap` write the precursor
  m/z with two decimals (0.01 Da steps); their fragments have three. In the other libraries at
  most 15% of precursors (GNPS) have two decimals or fewer. MS-DIAL low-resolution fragments use
  0-2 decimals.

### 5.3 Peaks

- Spectra with a peak more than 2 Da above the precursor: Enveda 24.5%, MoNA 42.4%, GNPS 30.8%,
  RIKEN 31.0%; under 13% elsewhere. Enveda's intensity above the precursor averages 1.1%.
- Enveda spectra are raw: 74% have a peak below 0.1% of the base, and the median spectrum keeps
  14 of its 138 peaks at the 1% floor.
- Three peaks or fewer: RIKEN 26.0%, MS-DIAL 17.8%, MassBank 16.7%; Enveda none.

### 5.4 Subformula coverage

- **Matching.** A peak counts as explained when it matches a precursor-bounded subformula of
  the labelled molecule. The candidate set is the one `filter_subformula_peaks` uses
  (charge migration included). The script re-checked 4,192 spectra against that function and
  found no disagreement.
- **Tolerance tiers.** The instrument tier uses the project's tolerances: ORB 10 ppm with a
  200 m/z floor, everything else 20 ppm with a 750 m/z floor (at least 15 mDa). The tight tier
  uses the ORB tolerance for all instruments.
- **Chance control.** The same match after shifting every peak by ±0.05 Da.
- **The instrument tier cannot check TOF data.** At 15 mDa, shifted peaks still match a median
  0.83 of Enveda intensity.
- **Dimers cannot be checked at all.** At the tight tier, chance still matches 0.985 of Enveda
  dimer intensity.
- **Tight tier, TOF/ORB single-charge monomers.** Median intensity explained, labelled formula
  vs chance control:

  | Library | Labelled formula | Chance control |
  | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | enveda-180 | 0.93 | 0.47 |
  | pluskal_ms2 | 0.98 | 0.43 |
  | gnps | 0.96 | 0.31 |
  | massbank | 1.00 | 0.14 |
  | mona | 0.89 | 0.22 |
  | spectraverse | 0.84 | 0.50 |
  | msdial | 0.91 | 0.21 |
  | enveda-np-examples | 0.90 | 0.31 |
  | masaryk | 0.99 | 0.33 |
  | riken | 0.52 | 0.10 |

  Only 35.5% of Enveda monomers exceed 0.95 tight coverage, and 59% sit at 0.80-0.95. That fits
  the raw sub-1% peaks of §5.3, but the tight tier has no 1%-floor variant to confirm it.
- **Suspect spectra.** A TOF/ORB monomer is suspect when its tight-tier base peak is unexplained
  and its coverage is within 0.1 of chance. 166,163 spectra are suspect; 116,448 of them are
  RIKEN rows labelled plain `QTOF` or `Orbitrap`. Counts and rates:

  | Library | Suspect spectra | Rate |
  | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | riken | 125,851 | 40.3% |
  | gnps | 14,365 | 7.0% |
  | mona | 8,660 | 10.1% |
  | massbank | 5,717 | 6.2% |
  | pluskal_ms2 | 4,280 | 0.81% |
  | spectraverse | 4,258 | 10.3% |
  | msdial | 1,937 | 13.6% |
  | enveda-180 | 1,067 | 0.13% |
  | masaryk | 22 | 6.2% |
  | enveda-np-examples | 6 | 0.5% |

  The flag marks candidates for wrong labels or contamination; it does not prove a spectrum is
  bad.
- **RIKEN, by label.**
  - **Plain `QTOF` (125,916 spectra):** tight coverage 0.075 against chance 0.070. The base peak
    is explained in 31% of spectra, and 40% have three peaks or fewer.
  - **Plain `Orbitrap` (97,425):** coverage 0.52 against chance 0.04. The base peak is explained
    in 52%.
  - **Both:** only `[M+H]+` and `[M+Na]+`, with a 10-80 eV ladder.
  - **Other labels** (`LC-ESI-QFT`, `LC-ESI-ITFT`, `LC-ESI-QTOF`): coverage 0.89-1.00.
  - **By precursor error:** RIKEN coverage falls from 0.77 at ≤5 ppm to 0.24 at 5-10 ppm and
    0.14 at 10-20 ppm.
- **Sodium adducts.** `[M+Na]+` has the lowest coverage of the common adducts: 0.57, with the
  base peak explained in 56% of spectra. RIKEN supplies 69% of the high-resolution `[M+Na]+`
  monomers. In the other libraries `[M+Na]+` coverage is 0.61-1.00.
- **Potassium adducts.** Orbitrap `[M+K]+` from GNPS, MoNA, RIKEN and Spectraverse
  (15,239 spectra) has coverage 0.37-0.43 and explains the base peak in only 26-39% of spectra.
  The precursor error is under 1 ppm, but these libraries store theoretical precursor m/z (§5.2).
- **Background ions.** In a sample of 13,243 Orbitrap spectra with an unexplained base peak, the
  base peak recurs at the same m/z across unrelated molecules: 173.54 (306 molecules, negative
  mode), 61.01 (188), 79.02 (176), 91.06 (152), 240.91 (134), 98.98, 61.99 and 96.96. These are
  background or cluster ions, not fragments.
- **Low-resolution spectra** (61,110) are matched at the QTOF tolerance. At the tight tier they
  explain 0.006-0.12 in GNPS, MassBank, MoNA, MS-DIAL and RIKEN, so treat them separately. The
  exception is Spectraverse (8,595 LR-labelled monomers, coverage 0.63), whose m/z values carry
  four decimals regardless of label.

### 5.5 Duplicates

- **Script:** [`scripts/debug/casmi26/casmi26_train_duplicates.py`](../../scripts/debug/casmi26/casmi26_train_duplicates.py),
  15 min on 8 workers. It writes `output/casmi26_train_check_2026_10_05/duplicates/`; the
  notebook's Section E reads it.
- **Exact copies** are byte-identical peak arrays (m/z and intensity), whatever the labels.
- **Near-duplicates** follow the [Spectraverse dedup rule](../plans/masster/spectraverse_dedup_plan.md)
  (user decisions 2026-10-04), reusing its pair code:
  - same InChIKey first block and adduct;
  - instrument class and collision energy match, and a missing value matches anything;
  - 5 ppm Hungarian cosine above 0.99;
  - `same` edges cluster and keep the spectrum with the most peaks, and a `wildcard` edge drops
    the less-specified spectrum.
- **The rule drops 446,352 of 2,539,608 spectra (17.6%)** and keeps all 275,810 molecules. It
  finds 1,042,818 edges: 702,963 `same` and 339,855 `wildcard`.

  | Library | Dropped | Share |
  | --- | ---: | ---: |
  | riken | 148,556 | 42.8% |
  | gnps | 114,817 | 52.0% |
  | pluskal_ms2 | 106,881 | 20.3% |
  | mona | 29,594 | 32.0% |
  | msdial | 26,783 | 65.7% |
  | massbank | 9,422 | 9.3% |
  | spectraverse | 7,045 | 13.8% |
  | enveda-180 | 2,832 | 0.2% |
  | drug_plus, masaryk, np-examples | 422 | |

- **What kind of duplicate each dropped spectrum is:**
  - 57,031 have a byte-identical twin, mostly in RIKEN and MS-DIAL (75% of MS-DIAL spectra have
    one).
  - 250,435 share their twin's m/z list but not its intensities: copies with rounded or rescaled
    intensities.
  - 138,886 are independent acquisitions (m/z differ, cosine above 0.99): replicates, which the
    rule also counts.
- **GNPS:** it has no CE, so 89,041 of its spectra drop as `wildcard` losers. They are mostly
  copies of pluskal, RIKEN, MoNA and MassBank spectra.
- **Label conflicts:** 6,805 byte-identical groups (23,980 spectra) carry more than one molecule.
  - 69% of the groups are one- or two-peak spectra that coincide by chance, mostly in RIKEN.
  - The 2,022 groups with at least five peaks (4,093 spectra, 3,549 in `pluskal_ms2`) are one
    acquisition labelled as two compounds. In 95.5% of them the precursors agree within 0.01 Da,
    and 97% have different formulas: co-isolated compounds, so at least one label in each group
    is wrong.
- **Overlap with §5.4:** of the 166,163 suspect spectra, 33,096 are dropped by the rule and
  14,472 have a byte-identical twin. Deduplication is not a substitute for the quality filter.

- **The adduct rule is a test-distribution question.** Every adduct in the placeholder test is

### 5.7 MSnLib (`pluskal_ms2`) vs NIST20/NIST23 HR

- **Script:** [`scripts/debug/casmi26/msnlib_nist_coverage.py`](../../scripts/debug/casmi26/msnlib_nist_coverage.py),
  2 min. It writes `output/msnlib_nist_coverage_2026_10_06/`.
- **Keys:** molecules are matched by 2D InChIKey first block, recomputed with RDKit from SMILES.
- **NIST rows:** HR MS/MS only (`nist20_hr`, `nist23_hr1/2`); MoNA rows are excluded.
- **Replication:** two blind recomputations reproduced the numbers, one exactly (RDKit keys) and
  one within 0.1% (stored keys; 49-59 stored NIST keys differ from RDKit's).

| Set | Molecules | In MSnLib | In NIST20 or NIST23 HR |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| MSnLib (`pluskal_ms2`) | 46,821 | | 6,432 (13.7%) |
| NIST20 HR | 26,800 | 4,980 (18.6%) | |
| NIST23 HR | 44,831 | 5,891 (13.1%) | |
| NIST20 or NIST23 HR | 46,717 | 6,432 (13.8%) | |
| `enveda-np-examples` | 250 | 224 | 219 (242 in either, 8 in neither) |

- **MSnLib and NIST are nearly disjoint.** Together they hold 87,106 molecules.
  - Murcko scaffolds: NIST covers 15% of MSnLib's, and MSnLib covers 37% of NIST's.
  - MSnLib-only molecules are larger (median 24 heavy atoms vs 17) and contain more sulfur (33%
    vs 21%).
- **NIST23 nearly contains NIST20:** 24,914 of NIST20's 26,800 molecules are in NIST23.
- **NIST would add 34,295 molecules** (73% of its HR set) that are in no CASMI train library.
- **Spectrum-level overlap is smaller than molecule overlap.**
  - On the 6,432 shared molecules, 7,890 of MSnLib's 11,739 (molecule, adduct) pairs also exist
    in NIST after adduct-name normalization. Formate is the largest gap (1,543 pairs).
  - 5,874 shared molecules have a NIST HCD `[M+H]+` or `[M-H]-` spectrum at NCE 15-75, MSnLib's
    range.
- **Acquisition differs.**
  - MSnLib is all Orbitrap, NCE 15-75, about 10 spectra per molecule, with formate and `[M+Cl]-`
    in negative mode.
  - NIST HR mixes ORB, ORB_F, QTOF, HCD and CID, about 31 spectra per molecule, and many
    `[M+H-H2O]+`, dimer and isotope precursors.
- **Spectraverse's MSnLib copy is a third of it:** 14,916 molecules, 94.9% of them in
  `pluskal_ms2`.
- **Not measured:** natural-product class coverage beyond the 250 np-examples, and stereo
  agreement (the 2D key is stereo-blind).

### 5.8 Molecule-distribution bias against the natural-product test

- **Script:** [`scripts/debug/casmi26/casmi26_mol_distribution.py`](../../scripts/debug/casmi26/casmi26_mol_distribution.py),
  4 min. It writes `output/casmi26_mol_distribution_2026_10_06/`.
- **Method:** Ertl NP-likeness from RDKit Contrib `NP_Score`, computed per molecule (RDKit 2D
  InChIKey block). A blind recomputation reproduced every headline number.
- **Train is synthetic-heavy.** NP median is -1.31, and 15.2% of molecules score above 0.
  - Enveda-180 is 66% of molecules and 45% of spectra, with NP median -1.49 and 1.0% above 0.
  - The rest of train has NP median -0.35, with 43% above 0.
  - The NP proxy `enveda-np-examples` has NP median 1.42, with 86% above 0.
- **The proxy looks different from train.** Against train it has more potential stereocentres
  (median 3 vs 1), more sugars (16% vs 7.8%) and fewer halogens (5% vs 30%).
- **By spectrum the bias is smaller:** 29.0% of spectra come from the 42,034 NP-like molecules,
  and the spectrum-weighted NP median is -1.05.
  - RIKEN supplies 41% of those spectra (NP median 1.13 per molecule).
  - `pluskal_ms2` (MSnLib) is drug-like (-1.35).
- **Nearest-neighbour coverage.** Morgan-2 Tanimoto to train, with the query molecules removed
  from every library:
  - np-examples: median 0.775, 73% at 0.7 or above, 14 below 0.5.
  - placeholder Enamine test: median 0.653.
  - `pluskal_ms2` sample: median 0.595.
  - All 250 np-examples are known compounds present in at least 2 other libraries (median 6), so
    an unpublished NP test is likely farther from train than this.
- **Quality filters fall on the NP-like data.**
  - 19.7% of NP-like spectra are subformula suspects (§5.4), against 1.2% of the rest.
  - 33.7% are near-duplicates (§5.5), against 11.0%.
  - 27.6% are RIKEN plain-`QTOF`/`Orbitrap` rows.
  - Dropping suspects and near-duplicates halves the NP-like spectra (735,586 to 373,695) but keeps
    40,497 of 42,034 NP-like molecules.
- **Instrument: partly confounded.** The test's timsTOF Pro 2 is a Q-TOF, and other libraries
  have plenty of NP-like Q-TOF data.
  - Outside Enveda-180 there are 265,106 NP-like TOF-class spectra of 25,021 molecules: RIKEN
    139k, GNPS 36k, MassBank 29k, MoNA 26k, Spectraverse 25k.
  - 42.5% of those spectra are RIKEN plain-`QTOF` rows.
  - After the quality filter and dedup, 137,349 spectra of 23,281 molecules remain.
  - CE units are eV for 155k of them, unknown for 73k and V for 37k.
  - The vendor match is thin: NP-like Bruker spectra (timsTOF, maXis, impact) outside Enveda-180
    total 8,891 spectra of 3,736 molecules. Enveda-180 is the only large Bruker timsTOF source,
    and `enveda-np-examples` (250 molecules, 1,184 spectra) the only NP-like timsTOF set.
- **Spectra per molecule:** median 6, maximum 1,468. The top 1% of molecules hold 17.4% of
  spectra.
- **Fingerprint caveat:** the CASMI `spectraverse` subset is lipid-heavy. 67% of its molecules
  have a Tanimoto-1.0 neighbour with a different InChIKey, because Morgan-2 cannot separate
  acyl-chain homologues.
- **Not measured:** compound classes (ClassyFire or NPClassifier, which need network access).

### 5.9 What the `riken` library is, and why part of it is noisy

**The noisy block is BMDMS-NP, not RIKEN data.**

- **Record-level match.** 125,936 of 125,936 rows labelled plain `QTOF` and 96,540 of 97,824 labelled
  plain `Orbitrap` have an exact m/z list (3 decimals) in RIKEN's redistribution of BMDMS-NP:
  - the file is `MSMS-Pos-bmdms-np_20200811.msp`, MS-DIAL MSP kit, Zenodo record 10951913,
    md5 `343befff3e53e6397c5002edc5c584ea`;
  - it holds 227,307 records, every one with `origin=BMDMS-NP`;
  - in a 5,000-row sample, 98.8% of matches also have equal intensities;
  - RIKEN's other labels match 0.3%.
- **The library.** BMDMS-NP is a Korean library of commercial plant metabolites (Lee S. et al.,
  *Phytochemistry* 177:112427, 2020, [doi:10.1016/j.phytochem.2020.112427](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.phytochem.2020.112427);
  only the abstract was read: 2,739 metabolites, multiple collision energies, repeated measurements).
- **Its design in the file:**
  - Orbitrap and QTOF;
  - `[M+H]+` and `[M+Na]+` only;
  - CE 10/20/40/60/80, plus 100/120/140 on Orbitrap;
  - precursor m/z is the theoretical value rounded to 2 decimals (70-80% of rows), so the
    precursor field cannot confirm the label.
- **Provenance route.** CASMI's `riken` bucket most likely came from RIKEN's MS-DIAL kit, an
  aggregator. BMDMS-NP is also in GNPS and MoNA, and Kaggle's provenance text was not readable.

**Why it is noisy** (measured on CASMI rows by a workflow, with an independent re-check):

- **Unmerged repeat scans.** Each (molecule, adduct, CE) has about 5 QTOF or 2 Orbitrap spectra
  (QTOF median 5, p99 17), and they are a mixture of good scans and noise scans.
  - Good scans: the precursor peak is within 3 mDa of theory. Median tight coverage is 0.61 (QTOF)
    and 0.83 (Orbitrap), base peak explained in 70-74%.
  - QTOF scans without the precursor peak sit at chance. Orbitrap scans without it stay somewhat
    above chance.
  - Among QTOF condition groups of 3 or more spectra: 6.7% all good, 23.5% mixed, 33.5% all noise.
- **What a noise scan contains:**
  - Ions above the precursor: 48.7% of QTOF spectra have a peak more than 2 Da above it, and
    21.6% have the base peak there.
  - Single peaks that recur across unrelated molecules (background).
  - On Orbitrap, off-grid side-peaks within ±1 Da of the precursor. The first record (chrysin,
    10 eV) shows 255.061 and 255.111 around the 255.093 precursor.
- **Noise tracks weak precursor signal.** QTOF noise is about 85% for low-polarity (TPSA < 20) or
  small (< m/z 200) molecules, against 35-45% for large polar ones. It is the same for `[M+H]+` and
  `[M+Na]+`.
- **Ruled out as the main cause:**
  - mass calibration: matched-peak errors are a sharp spike at 0, with no offset;
  - the 2-decimal precursor rounding;
  - literature-transcribed or in-silico spectra;
  - `[M+Na]+` mislabels.
- **Wrong compound, partly:** a minority of noise scans (3-8%) are spectra of another library
  compound.

**The rest of `riken`** is measured standards in MassBank / MoNA / MS-DIAL copies, mostly clean
(tight coverage 0.89-1.00) and largely removed by dedup (§5.5). RIKEN's own resources match none of
the BMDMS-NP fingerprint:

- PlaSMA: Waters Xevo G2 Q-Tof, 10/30/50 eV, per MoNA records;
- ReSpect: Sawada et al., *Phytochemistry* 82:38-45, 2012 — 76% of its 3,595 metabolites are
  digitised from 163 literature reports;
- NPDepo.

The low-resolution ion-trap and QqQ rows (about 17k) are the ones to drop on precision.

**Is the noise one shared foreign signal? Mostly no.**

- **Script:** [`scripts/debug/casmi26/riken_bmdms_shared_signal.py`](../../scripts/debug/casmi26/riken_bmdms_shared_signal.py),
  8 min. It writes `output/riken_bmdms_shared_signal_2026_10_06/`, and the BMDMS-NP MSP is kept in
  `cache/` there.
- **Test:** for each scan with at least 3 peaks, the closest scan of a molecule with a different
  formula (0.01 Da binned sqrt-intensity cosine). A twin is a match with cosine at least 0.9.
- **Twins are not specific to noise.** 15.3% of noise scans (12,499 of 81,636) have a twin, and
  18.7% of good scans do. Good-scan twins are mostly related compounds, with a median precursor
  gap of 30 Da.
- **It is not co-isolation.** Only 17% of noise twins have a precursor within 1.5 Da; the median
  gap is 5.1 Da.
- **It is run-local.** Noise twins are a median 239 BMDMS-NP records apart, against 1,389 for
  good-scan twins and 90,618 for random pairs. That suggests carry-over or background shared
  between neighbouring injections, assuming record order follows acquisition.
- **2,421 noise scans match a good scan of another compound:** a real spectrum under the wrong
  label.
- **35,066 noise scans have fewer than 3 peaks** and were not tested.
- **Consequence:** cross-molecule dedup does not remove the noise; the quality filter below does.

**Filter used to keep the good BMDMS-NP scans.** The thresholds are the workflow's proposal and
are not tested on a model. Keep a scan only if all of these hold:

- tight-tier base peak explained;
- tight coverage ≥ 0.5;
- coverage more than 0.3 above the chance control;
- at least 4 peaks.

Without the peak-count rule, single-peak spectra pass by chance. The rule keeps 37,157 spectra of
2,469 molecules. 61-69% of the block's molecules also appear in another library.
